Why it reads whole words

Many Chinese characters have more than one reading, and the character alone does not tell you which. The converter finds the word each character belongs to and uses that word's reading:

ChinesePinyinMeaning
銀行yín hángbank
行走xíng zǒuwalk
長大zhǎng dàgrow up
長度cháng dùlength
睡著了shuì zháo lefell asleep
看著你kàn zhe nǐlooking at you

How to use it

  1. Paste Chinese into the box. The pinyin appears below as you type.
  2. To practise sentence by sentence, paste a whole passage (a lesson, a dialogue or song lyrics) and turn on Line by line: each Chinese line is followed by its pinyin.
  3. Switch how tones are shown at the top: tone marks (nǐ hǎo), numbers (ni3 hao3) or none (ni hao). In number mode the neutral tone is 5.

FAQ

Does it work with both Traditional and Simplified Chinese?

Yes, and you can mix them. Traditional characters are mapped to their Simplified forms behind the scenes to look up the reading; the original text you pasted is what stays on screen.

Does it get characters with more than one reading right?

Mostly. The reading is chosen by the whole word: 行 is háng in 銀行 (bank) but xíng in 行走 (walk), and 長 is zhǎng in 長大 (grow up) but cháng in 長度 (length). Rare combinations missing from the word list can still come out wrong.

Which standard does it follow?

Taiwan's standard Mandarin, as in the Ministry of Education dictionary. Common words read differently across the Taiwan Strait use the Taiwan reading — 垃圾 is lè sè, 星期 is xīng qí — and the neutral tone is kept only for particles like 的, 了, 嗎 and 們, so 朋友 is péng yǒu. Less common words may occasionally still follow the mainland reading.

Are the tone changes of 一 and 不 shown?

Yes, as they are actually pronounced: 一起 is yì qǐ, 一個 is yí gè and 不是 is bú shì, so you can read it aloud as written.

Can I use it for song lyrics?

Yes. Paste the whole song and turn on Line by line: each Chinese line is followed by its pinyin, with tone changes marked as they are sung.
