Why it needs a dictionary

Each kana stands for one sound, so kana can be converted with a table. Kanji cannot: the same character is read differently in different words, and only a dictionary knows which. The converter splits the sentence into words and uses each word's reading:

How to use it

  1. Paste Japanese into the box. The romaji appears below as you type. The first time, wait a few seconds for the dictionary to download.
  2. To practise sentence by sentence, paste a whole passage (a lesson, a dialogue or song lyrics) and turn on Line by line: each Japanese line is followed by its romaji.
  3. Switch how long vowels are written at the top: as spelled (arigatou) or with macrons (arigatō).

FAQ

Why is は written wa and を written o?

As particles, は is pronounced wa, へ is e and を is o, and the romaji follows the pronunciation, as in Hepburn. Some lyrics sites write を as wo; both spellings are common and sound the same.

What is the difference between As spelled and Macrons?

Only how long vowels are written. As spelled follows the kana letter by letter: ありがとう is arigatou and 東京 is toukyou. Macrons marks long vowels with ō and ū: arigatō and tōkyō. They are pronounced the same.

Why does the first conversion take a moment?

Kanji readings come from a dictionary of about 17 MB that has to download to your browser the first time. After that your browser keeps it. Everything runs in your browser; your text is never sent to a server.

Are the kanji readings always right?

Everyday words mostly are. Personal names, place names, and special readings a lyricist assigns in song lyrics (the small kana printed next to the kanji) get the ordinary reading, which may differ from what is sung.

Can I use it for song lyrics?

Yes. Paste the whole song and turn on Line by line: each Japanese line is followed by its romaji. Special readings a lyricist assigns get the ordinary reading, so go with what is sung when they differ.
